# Learning from the author's own texts

Texts the author wrote without AI (pasted, or given as files) show their voice
without the pull of an AI draft. They are a source of observations and of
exemplars.

## 1. Confirm authorship

Use a text only if the author wrote it without AI help. If that is unclear,
ask once. Texts that started from an AI draft are revisions, not own texts.

## 2. Label and classify each text

For each text: a label that tells it apart (what it is, for whom, roughly
when), its language, topic and audience. A text on a personal topic, or to
family or friends, stays in the global store.

## 3. Record the traits

- A trait shared by several of the given texts is one observation with one
  source per text, so its evidence is the number of texts that show it.
- A distinctive trait in a single text is an observation with evidence 1.
- State each trait in English with a verbatim quote; skip traits the model
  shows anyway.
- Source kind: `own text`.

## 4. Keep exemplars

From each text, you may keep one excerpt that shows the author's voice well:

- At most 300 words, verbatim; never alter the author's wording.
- Choose a passage without client or project names and without other
  people's contact details. Exemplars live in the global store and guide texts
  in every project, so a name in them would carry one project's material into
  the others. If no passage of the text is free of them, keep no exemplar from
  that text: the observations still record its traits.
- Only in the global store, as `exemplars/<slug>.<code>.<nn>.md`, with the
  frontmatter from the store format (topic, language, audience, source, date,
  word count).
- At most five per topic and language. When five already exist, do not add
  one; mention in the notice that maintenance can replace an older one.

Then go back to the workflow in `SKILL.md`: route and record.
